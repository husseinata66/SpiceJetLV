### The distinction that governs every number above

**No case in either suite has been *executed as a test case*.** Every case carries `Review: Pending`,
and Stage 4M consumes a case only once `Review = Approved`. In pipeline terms, **zero have run**.

`EXECUTED` means *Stage 2 Discovery already produced the evidence this case asks for*. The checks came
first; the cases were written afterwards to describe what was and was not checked. That is a real
result - it is not a signed-off test run, and the two must not be conflated in any report upward.

## 7. Execution guidance - what to re-run, and what not to

Most of the exercised cases do **not** need re-running before approval. The evidence is paired,
timestamped and archived, and repeating identical checks on the same bookings adds nothing. Three
categories differ.

### 7.1 Re-run before approval (~8 cases) - single observations a developer will be asked to reproduce

A defect report resting on one sighting tends to return as *cannot reproduce*.

| What | Why re-run |
|---|---|
| Passenger with no ticket (`FINDING-R13`) | Seen once. **Cannot be re-run on that booking - it is already exchanged.** Needs the next exchanges watched |
| All ticket numbers lost (`FINDING-R14`) | Seen once, and the **next** exchange contradicted it - something conditional is happening and the condition is unknown |
| Wallet overcharge (`FINDING-R18`) | Measured once. Confirm whether it always rounds up or only sometimes |
| Opaque 409 on book (`FINDING-R07`) | Occurred once by accident; never triggered deliberately |
| Eligibility on a bad credential (`FINDING-R22`) | **Explicitly unreproduced.** The untested step that matters most is a credential belonging to a *different agency* |
| Merged fare groups (`FINDING-R08`) | Found by reading payloads, never tested with real split-fare data |

### 7.2 Do not re-run (~50 cases)

Anything verified across several bookings or several conditions - the base fare and tax mapping across
18 offers at two exchange rates, the rounding defect at two rates, the lost ticket numbers across four
bookings. More of the same proves nothing new.

**But note the weaker subset:** a few cases marked `EXECUTED` were verified *incidentally rather than
deliberately*. The flight number, for instance, was only ever checked because it is the key offers are
aligned by - nobody set out to test it. Those deserve one honest pass.

### 7.3 Mandatory after fixes - all 29 failing cases

This is the reason the suites exist. Every failing case is re-run once its fix lands, and must then
pass. Without that, a fix is trusted because someone said so.

### 7.4 A real cost to weigh

**Every exchange spends real money.** Re-running the ticketing and wallet cases means paying for more
exchanges. Whether confirming a 0.12 overcharge is worth another full fare is a commercial decision,
not a QA one.

only a human reviewer may change either. Stage 4M consumes a case only when `Review = Approved`;
Stage 4A only when `Review = Approved` **and** `Automation = Approved`.
